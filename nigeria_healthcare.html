04 / What the snapshot reveals

The gaps are
part of the story.

These findings describe the full national snapshot. Interactive cohort filters do not change these national conclusions.

01

Nearly half the operating picture is unknown.

49.1%

34,496 records have blank or unknown operating status. They must not be classified as closed or non-functional.

Next step

Validate status before estimating service availability or making referral decisions.

02

A point map leaves records out.

11,706

16.7% of records lack coordinates. Lagos has the largest number of unmapped records (882), while a percentage ranking answers a different question.

Next step

Pair map views with full registry counts and a geocoding verification list.

03

A large registry still needs a quality audit.

42.7%

29,976 records carry one or more source quality flags. A flag indicates a record for review; it is not proof that a facility is invalid.

Next step

Investigate flagged records with source documentation and local validation; retain them in this analysis.

The denominator lesson

99.1% can tell
an incomplete story.

35,434 records are labeled “Functional”: 99.1% of the 35,739 records with known status, but only 50.5% of all 70,235 records. Neither percentage establishes actual service availability. Missingness changes the interpretation.

Cleaning & quality decisions

Verify complete, non-overlapping pagination and unique source identifiers. Trim administrative labels and group by state plus LGA. Standardize blank ownership and care level to Unknown. Retain blank/unknown operating status separately from known status.

All 70,235 records are retained, including 11,706 without coordinates and 29,976 source-flagged records. No globally invalid coordinates were found among non-null pairs. 1,336 repeated-name groups contain 1,378 excess records; matching names alone are insufficient evidence to delete a facility.

Questions this project can—and cannot—answer

It can compare recorded facility counts, care-level/ownership composition, known-status coverage, and data-quality gaps. It cannot establish service capacity, clinical quality, staff availability, population-adjusted need, journey time, or affordability.

The publisher describes the release as non-exhaustive and not fully validated. Source inputs span different dates. A record is not a guaranteed distinct, open, accessible facility. State totals and map centroids are descriptive summaries, not a basis for allocating care on their own.

Reproduce the analysis

Download the Python pipeline, run python healthcare_analysis.py --download, then inspect the generated aggregate JSON and CSV files. It uses Python's standard library and SQLite. Compare the raw SHA-256 checksum in source.json to identify source revisions.
